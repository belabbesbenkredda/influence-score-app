What the public cares about, against what it got

Filled marks are the share of Americans naming a topic as the country's most important problem (Gallup). Hollow marks are the share of the corpus devoted to it, counted proportionally. The line between them is the gap — the distance between public concern and public discourse, which is the whole point of measuring salience separately.

Outlets

The influence of one representative item, not a period total: sampling is capped per outlet, so a total would rank outlets by how many of their items we could fetch. An outlet dominating the leaderboard is a finding, not a fault.

Method

  1. R — a sourced third-party audience figure, converted to people reaching one item and divided by US adults. Absolute: no normalisation against a category leader, no platform weights.
  2. S — Gallup's Most Important Problem, applied proportionally across every topic an item addresses.
  3. D — Logos, Ethos and Pathos, each 0–10, rubric score_v2. Ethos is the speaker's standing with their own audience, never their fairness: the instrument is descriptive, and must register a demagogue and a statesman alike.
  4. Points — I × 1,000, the expected influence per thousand American adults.

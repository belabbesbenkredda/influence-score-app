The assumptions inside R

Every medium reports a different currency — viewers, visits, listeners, subscribers. Converting them into one quantity (people reaching one item) needs the constants below. None of them is measured; each is a stated assumption, gathered in psi/audience.py so it can be replaced one at a time. The digital divisor is the weakest.

RADIO_EPISODES_PER_WEEK5weekly cume divided across this many episodes
PODCAST_EPISODE_VIEW_RATE0.1share of subscribers watching a given episode
PODCAST_AUDIO_MULTIPLE1.0audio listeners per video viewer
NEWSLETTER_OPEN_RATE0.4share of subscribers opening an issue
DIGITAL_ITEMS_PER_MONTH800articles per month, to split monthly visits
TV_SEGMENT_SHARE1.0share of a programme's average audience reached by one segment

Denominator: 262,000,000 US adults — US Census Bureau resident population 18+, 2024 vintage (rounded).

Method, in eight lines

  1. Universe: 124 national outlets across six media; 108 carry a sourced third-party audience figure, each with a URL, a verbatim quote and a date.
  2. R: the outlet's audience figure converted to people reaching one item, divided by US adults. Leader-normalisation and platform weights are gone — R is now absolute.
  3. S: Gallup's Most Important Problem (2026-08), applied proportionally across every topic an item addresses.
  4. D: Logos + Ethos + Pathos, each 0-10, from rubric score_v2. Ethos is the speaker's standing with their own audience, never their fairness — the framework must catch both a demagogue and a statesman.
  5. I = R × S × D per item; outlets are the mean of their items.
  6. Corpus: 581 items published 2026-08-20 to 2026-09-03, fetched from RSS, article pages and published transcripts.
  7. Known gap: the corpus is concentrated in a few days and skewed towards outlets that permit automated fetching. Paywalled papers and untranscribed audio are under-represented.
  8. Nothing is estimated silently: unsourced reach is null, and every modelling constant is named above.
